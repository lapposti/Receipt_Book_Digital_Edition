Explanation of Metadata
The “Receipt Book” is a collection of recipes dating back to the 1700s.  “The Recipes Project” includes the work of Sara 
Pennell who is a historian that has focused on food culture during the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. I interpreted 
her perspective as one that emphasizes the kitchen as a historical place, so in approaching my metadata I wanted to 
understand what the recipes can provide as context for what was going on in England during the time period. The first
column is the individual file name so that I would be able to orientate myself as I gathered the rest of the information. For 
The second column I gave each file a Unique ID which consisted of “MS2096” followed by its corresponding number. This 
reflected the Ms. Codex which is also the third column. This helps identify the source and shelf mark within the centers catalogue. 
The physical book had different page numbers than what the library assigned to it so I thought the page orientation would be important
so that I would be able to understand the correlation between recipes if they went over the page. R stands for recto, right side of the page
and V stands for verso, left or backside of the page. The page numbers correlate to those assigned by the library. The recipe category is 
how I categorized the food that was mentioned in the recipe. This category I kept broad and without correlation for example between
“fruit” and “breakfast”. Since I only referred to tittles and not the actual recipe breakdown, I can further expand this category.  For key 
ingredients, I focused on what ingredient was mentioned in the title and for the last category, preparation method/form I listed what was
actually being done with the ingredient. I think these processes work together to explain the context and give a story about the person 
creating these dishes and what their intentions were in doing so. For the last four pages, since they are cover pages, the information 
wasn’t applicable but they tell their own stories as well. I included them because of the names, colors, and features that I think are 
important to my understanding of the book overall. 

Extract & Clean Text
During this process, I believe that the HTR for my text was overall accurate. There were minor grammatical errors and choices that ChatGPT made that caused other errors. I did minimal hand-cleaning, considering that the changes made were to letters I viewed differently or that the transcriptions were "too modern". For example, some of the text would use "ye" for phrases like "the" to fit modern understanding. Another error was that ChatGPT began to list the page numbers at the beginning of every transcription, but only some pages of the manuscript actually had page numbers so I would need to correct it. As I imputed pages it would also make up its own directions such as automatically making the transcription into a plain text file for every page I had uploaded which I believe caused it to make more mistakes. ChatGPT in comparison to Transkribus could understand the difference between the "e" and "d" in the manuscript, because the style of "d" had a curve that must have confused the software. There were multiple recipes listed on each page, and some would even continue on to the next page and ChatGPT did an impressive job of listing the recipes on separate lines and adding ellipsis if the receipt was cut off. It was also keeping accurate track of page numbers according to how I titled them which made it convenient in terms of where I was in transcription. There was also a lot of repetition in the process of recipe making that I didn't notice initially. Since I had limited Transkribus credits, what I focused on was highlighting the symbols that were frequent throughout the manuscript but I couldn't understand like "ye", as preciously mentioned. For my process of hand-correcting what I did was go line by line having the plain text and digital manuscript side by side and updated any differences I noticed. I would consult the original ChatGPT upload if there was something I still didn't understand and couldn't identify on my own. The handwriting style also differs throughout the book in darkness so I had to pay closer attention during some pages. In the ethical approach of this work, I believe that the original message gets lost in translation. Some of the decisions made by the computer were different from my own, such as phrasing and spacing. For example, ChatGPT added multiple periods after a sentence/paragraph ended. This punctuation doesn't exist in the origional manuscript, but for my own understanding I choose to keep it in the transcription throughout the recipe. Someone else who is reviewing this work might disagree with my decision and that can cause a discrepancy. I think perception bias heavily exists in this work. Something that is careful in this work is definitely the understanding of the harms but I think it's something that also gets overlooked. From this experience what I found most interesting was how sudden I was able to identify words once I saw a digital transcript right next to the digital copy of the manuscript compared to how much I was struggling doing that process in reverse during my initial categorization of the receipts. Each person that interacts with this work needs to take the care and time to acknowledge their own bias and contributions in order to help the next person because I don't think there is a reasonable approach yet that holds everyone accountable, so its best to start with yourself.
